# Source references and acquisition

1. Verner, D. A., Ferland, G. J., Korista, K. T. & Yakovlev, D. G. (1996), **Atomic Data for Astrophysics. II. New Analytic Fits for Photoionization Cross Sections of Atoms and Ions**, *Astrophysical Journal* 465, 487, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1086/177435. Original author site https://www.pa.uky.edu/~verner/photo.html; archived arXiv astro-ph/9601009. Defines fitted source cross-sections reused through immutable PHYS09, **not** the unknown electron-impact secondary cross section.
2. NIST Atomic Spectra Database (ASD) v5.12, DOI https://doi.org/10.18434/T4W30F, helium I 24.587389011 eV, helium II 54.4177655282 eV: https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/ASD/ie.pl?at_num_out=on&biblio=on&e_out=0&el_name_out=on&level_out=on&seq_out=on&shells_out=on&spectra=He&unc_out=on&units=1. PHYS11 computation **keeps original source threshold 24.59 eV**, not replacing it with NIST.
3. Samson, J. A. R., Stolte, W. C., He, Z.-X., Cutler, J. N., Lu, Y. & Bartlett, R. J. (1998), **Double photoionization of helium**, *Physical Review A* 57, 1906–1911, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.57.1906. Absolute double photoionization is measured from roughly 79 eV threshold up to 820 eV; all PHYS10 original photon energies are <35 eV, so that table must not be extrapolated below its threshold.
4. Shah, M. B., Elliott, D. S. & Gilbody, H. B. (1987), **Pulsed crossed-beam study of the ionisation of atomic hydrogen by electron impact**, *Journal of Physics B* 20, 3501–3514, DOI: https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3700/20/14/022. Relevant primary experimental total cross-sections for 13.6–20.99 eV electron-impact H I channel; **not used numerically** here.
5. Childers, J. G., James, K. E., Hughes, M., Bray, I., Baertschy, M. & Khakoo, M. A. (2003), **Electron-impact ionization of atomic hydrogen at incident electron energies of 15.6, 17.6, 25, and 40 eV**, *Physical Review A* 68, 030702(R), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.68.030702. Differential EII measurements; not yet supplied as source-owned transport kernel.
6. NIST Standard Reference Database 107: Kim, Y.-K. et al., **Electron-Impact Cross Sections for Ionization and Excitation Database**, DOI: https://doi.org/10.18434/T4KK5C; https://www.nist.gov/pml/electron-impact-cross-sections-ionization-and-excitation-database. Atomic H and He models, energy distributions; available as model/data source after explicit source crosswalk and uncertainty/transport assessment.

No user-supplied document is **required** to reproduce current PHYS11 energy-only theorem and data. For actual source-owned secondary collision probabilities / physical K: require provenance-pinned numerical H e-impact σ(E), differential energy sharing near threshold, and electron propagation/stopping/target history on same proper-time geometry, including strict model validity; a paper alone does not furnish the latter.
