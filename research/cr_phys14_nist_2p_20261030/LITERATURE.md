# Original-source literature and provenance

1. NIST Electron-Impact Cross Section Database, H(1s)->2p numeric excitation table, https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/Ionization/excit_data.php?final=2p&id=HI&initial=1s&ision=E (viewed 2026-10-30). The BE-scaled column is **theoretical**, not direct observation. Reported values in 10^-16 cm^2; converted exactly with factor 1e-20 m^2. Current NIST database lists 1s->2p excitation threshold 10.1988 eV. The source table was transcribed into `nist_h_2p_cross_sections.csv`; its SHA256 is locked. We did not acquire or claim raw NIST webpage byte identity.
2. P. M. Stone, Y.-K. Kim, J. P. Desclaux (2002), *Electron-Impact Cross Sections for Dipole- and Spin-Allowed Excitations of Hydrogen, Helium, and Lithium*, J. Res. NIST **107**, 327, DOI https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.107.026. BE-scaled Born method; finite energy grid, smooth threshold, no resonances. Original paper reports H 1s->2p energy ~10.204 eV; PHYS14 follows current NIST database 10.1988 eV. **Do not silently equate model BE-scaled values and CCC/experiment.**
3. M. B. Shah, D. S. Elliott, H. B. Gilbody (1987), *Pulsed crossed-beam study of the ionisation of atomic hydrogen by electron impact*, J. Phys. B **20**, 3501–3514, DOI https://doi.org/10.1088/0022-3700/20/14/022. Original physical measured 26 points, individual reproducibility evaluated at 67% and separate 6.8% absolute scale; inherited byte-identically from PHYS13 SOURCE ZIP. Raw user PDF hash in SOURCE_IDENTITY, not redistributed.
4. Y.-K. Kim (1992), *Compact Fitting Formulas for Electron-Impact Cross Sections*, J. Res. NIST **97**, 689–692, DOI https://doi.org/10.6028/jres.097.032. Physical fit to Shah measured ionization; not substitute for raw measurements.
5. I. Bray, A. W. Bray, D. V. Fursa, A. S. Kadyrov (2018), *Near-Threshold Cross Sections for Electron and Positron Impact Ionization of Atomic Hydrogen*, PRL **121**, 203401, DOI https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.121.203401. Wannier behavior in the near-threshold domain, without constructing new PHYS14 measured values.
